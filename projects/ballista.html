Project

High-Performance Ballista

A wooden torsion ballista built for a high school physics competition against roughly 30 other teams. Kinematics predicted about 60 m of range. It threw 35 m, and the gap turned out to be the most useful part of the project. Placed 1st.

CompetitionHigh school physics, ~30 teams of 2–3
Result1st place
Mass6 kg (10 kg limit)
EnvelopeWithin 50 × 50 × 60 cm
MechanismTorsion bundle, wooden frame
ProjectileGolf ball
Range35 m measured (60 m predicted)

01Design & Constraints

The competition set hard limits on size, mass, and manufacturing method. Everything had to be built by hand.

  • No CNC manufacturing
  • Under 10 kg, built at 6 kg
  • Within 50 × 50 × 60 cm
  • Golf ball projectile
A torsion bundle stores the energy: twisted cord loaded by rotating the arms back against it, released to drive the sled forward along the trough. I designed the mechanism and geometry, two teammates cut parts, and I led the assembly.

02Drawings

Hand-drawn layouts used to cut and assemble the frame. Top and side views, dimensioned in centimetres.

03Prediction vs. Result

The range prediction came from kinematics, treating the launch as lossless with all stored energy going into the ball. That gave about 60 m. The ballista threw 35 m, tape-measured at the competition.

Where the energy went. At the end of the stroke the arms struck the frame hard enough to crack audibly. That is energy going into the structure rather than the projectile, and the lossless model had no way to account for it. A launch model that matched the real machine would need to carry the energy left in the arms at release, plus friction in the trough and losses in the cord.
